Follow the gap

Still in test

When a share opens sharply higher or lower on news, trade in that direction for the rest of the day. It is on practice money. Its last check is read on 28 December 2026, and we’ll say what it shows either way.

Tested and retired

1. Opening-range breakout

Buy when a share breaks out of its first few minutes’ range.

Trading costs took almost everything it made.

Costs ate it

2. Back to the average

Bet that a share which runs away from its average price for the day comes back.

Small wins, full-size losses. No better than entering at random.

No better than chance

3. Fade the gap

Bet that a share which opens sharply up or down reverses.

Looked good on three months, then lost on six months it had never seen. We’d picked a lucky stretch.

Didn’t hold up

4. Late-day momentum on the big index funds

A published academic finding that the last half hour follows the first.

The move is real but too small to pay for trading it at our size.

Costs ate it

5. The same rules on triple-leveraged funds

More movement, same rules.

The extra movement went into stopped-out trades, not profit.

No better than chance

6. Crypto within the day

The same approach on Bitcoin and Ether.

Lost before costs and after, and each trade cost about nine times as much as a share trade.

Lost before costs

7. Hold for three days after a sharp fall

From a trading reel.

Made a little after costs, but missed our pass mark in both halves of the test.

Missed the pass mark

8. A big morning move the market doesn’t share

Trade a share that moves hard in the first half hour on its own.

Lost before costs. An early wobble isn’t news the way an overnight gap is.

Lost before costs

9. The gap rule on forty other shares

The rule still in test, unchanged, on names it wasn’t found on.

Made nothing. A warning we’re keeping in view for the rule itself.

Didn’t hold up

10. Cheap options after a sharp fall

The reel’s actual trade, tested on real option prices.

Most tickets lost more than half their cost, and days after a fall did no better than random days.

No better than chance

Studies, not trades

S1. Do shares bounce after a sharp fall?

A study of prices, not a trade.

Yes, over about three days. But both ways of trading it (numbers 7 and 10) failed.

Study

S2. Is the gap rule really about company results?

A study of where the rule’s past trades sat.

Not shown either way. Too few trades to tell.

Study

S3. Does the rule lean on buying or on selling short?

A study of the rule’s past trades, split by direction.

The buying trades did better than the short-selling ones. It is a look back, not a new test, and nothing changes yet.

Study
