My Robot Trader In testing

What we tested, and what happened.

Thirteen ideas: ten retired, two studies, one still in test. No figures until the test is done.

Still being tested (1)

  • Follow the gap. When a share opens sharply higher or lower on news, trade in that direction for the rest of the day. It is on practice money. Its last check is read on 28 December 2026, and we’ll say what it shows either way.

Tested and retired (10)

  1. Opening-range breakout. Buy when a share breaks out of its first few minutes’ range. Trading costs took almost everything it made.
  2. Back to the average. Bet that a share which runs away from its average price for the day comes back. Small wins, full-size losses. No better than entering at random.
  3. Fade the gap. Bet that a share which opens sharply up or down reverses. Looked good on three months, then lost on six months it had never seen. We’d picked a lucky stretch.
  4. Late-day momentum on the big index funds. A published academic finding that the last half hour follows the first. The move is real but too small to pay for trading it at our size.
  5. The same rules on triple-leveraged funds. More movement, same rules. The extra movement went into stopped-out trades, not profit.
  6. Crypto within the day. The same approach on Bitcoin and Ether. Lost before costs and after, and each trade cost about nine times as much as a share trade.
  7. Hold for three days after a sharp fall. From a trading reel. Made a little after costs, but missed our pass mark in both halves of the test.
  8. A big morning move the market doesn’t share. Trade a share that moves hard in the first half hour on its own. Lost before costs. An early wobble isn’t news the way an overnight gap is.
  9. The gap rule on forty other shares. The rule still in test, unchanged, on names it wasn’t found on. Made nothing. A warning we’re keeping in view for the rule itself.
  10. Cheap options after a sharp fall. The reel’s actual trade, tested on real option prices. Most tickets lost more than half their cost, and days after a fall did no better than random days.

Studies, not trades (2)

  • Do shares bounce after a sharp fall? Yes, over about three days. But both ways of trading it (numbers 7 and 10) failed.
  • Is the gap rule really about company results? Not shown either way. Too few trades to tell.
